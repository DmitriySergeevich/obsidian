## Core idea
How close the result is to now and to expectations.

---
## Words

**already** - earlier than expected. Use before past participle or in the end. Goes before past participle or at the end of sentences

**yet** - not done. Goes at the end of sentence or phrase. Use with negative or question

**just** - very short time before and very close to now. Goes before past participle

**recently** - very short time before but not very close to now. Goes before past participle or at the beginning or at the end of sentences 

**still** - action haven't happened, but we expect to happen in the future. Goes before have/has

---
## Example:
- I've just finished
- Have you done it yet?
