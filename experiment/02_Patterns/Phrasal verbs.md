## Core idea
The combination a verb and a particle (a preposition or an adverb) has a new meaning of the verb alone.

---
## Separable phrasal verbs
In these phrasal verbs we can separate verb and particle
### put on
- to cover part of the body with clothes, shoes, make-up, etc.
- to make a device operate
##### Examples
- I put on my hat. /  I put my hat on.
- Could you put the light on? / Could you put on the light?
---
### put away
- to put something in the place or container
- to put someone into a place where people live, such as a mental hospital. Also in slang meaning to send someone to pirson
- to eat large amount of food
##### Examples
- Put away your toys. / Put your toys away.
- He put away a whole box of chocolate. / He put whole box of chocolate away.
---
### pick up
- to lift someone or something using your hand
- to collect someone or something
- to learn a new skill or language by practice in rather than being taught it
##### Examples
- We picked up our rubbish. /  We picked our rubbish up.
- Could you pick up the books I ordered?
- When you live in a country you soon pick up language.
---
### take off 
- to remove something, especially clothes
- to spend time away from your work
##### Examples
- I'll take off my jacket. / I'll take my jacket off.
- He took two weeks off in September. / He took off two weeks in September.
---
### other examples
- throw away - to waste a skill or opportunity. Put rubbish in container 
- switch on - to make start working of something
- write down - to write something on a peace of paper
- shut down -  to make stop working of something
- look up - to try to find a peace of information. Visit someone.
- try on - to put on a piece of clothing to discover if it fits you or if you like it
---
### Notes
If the object is a pronoun, the pronoun always comes before the particle.

---
## Inseparable phrasal verbs
In these phrasal verbs we cannot separate verb and particle

### look after
- to take care of or be in charge of someone or something:
##### Examples
- I look after my children

---
### get on
- to go onto a bus, train, aircraft, or boat
- to have a good relationship
- to manage or deal with a situation, especially successfully
##### Examples
- I got on the bus.
- He doesn't get on with his daughter.
- We are getting on quite well with the decoration.

---
### Other examples
- come across - to find something by chance. to give other people a certain feeling or opinion
- stick with - to continue doing something
- log onto - log in to the system
- break into - quick and force go into house
